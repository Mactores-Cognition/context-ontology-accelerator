Minutes of wait budget, worst case

How long each step will wait before giving up

Every budget is an env-var override. Bars are the defaults, in minutes. Red bars exit the script on timeout; amber bars warn and continue into cdk destroy anyway.

Why connector stacks get 30 minutes

The COA-operated Databricks connector is VPC-attached. Its security group cannot be deleted until Lambda releases the function's ENIs, which can take up to 20 minutes. Giving up sooner leaves the group behind, and step 7 then fails to delete the VPC it sits in.

Why the AgentCore ENI wait is the one that stops everything

AgentCore Runtime ENIs are provisioned outside CloudFormation and are not waited on by CFN. They have been observed taking hours to detach after a runtime delete. While any remain attached, DeleteSecurityGroup fails with DependencyViolation — so continuing into cdk destroy would burn a long run to reach a guaranteed failure. The script exits instead and tells you to come back later.

The 55-minute figure

Sum of the five default budgets (30 + 10 + 5 + 5 + 3). It is the waiting ceiling only — it excludes cdk destroy --all itself, which has no budget here and is bounded by CloudFormation.

The seven steps, in order. On timeout is what the script does when the wait budget runs out — the difference between an exit and a warning is deliberate, and is the single most useful thing to know when a teardown stalls.
